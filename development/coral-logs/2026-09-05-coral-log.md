### 2 · Sub-deck view collapsed instead of filling the screen (rc.37)
Xian, reviewing the set: *"some are top aligned with the (+) floating
high and some are bottom aligned… maybe we need center-alignment in the
app?"* He offered it as a design choice. It was a bug, and the app
already wanted what he asked for.

The sub-deck branch wraps `SubstackView` in a `motion.div` (slide-in
animation) carrying **no flex classes**. As a flex child it defaults to
sizing to its content, so `SubstackView`'s own `flex-1` and
`TaskStack`'s `items-center justify-center` had nothing to fill or
centre against. The whole view collapsed to content height and sat at
the top. The main deck has no such wrapper and was centring correctly
all along.

One line (`flex flex-col flex-1 min-h-0`). Every view now centres its
card with "+" pinned at the bottom — covenant 1 says a card is a card
at every depth, and the layout finally says so too. This also **retires
the "sub-deck top-aligns" note I had filed as a post-1.0 design
question**: it was never a decision anyone needed to make.

Set re-shot against rc.37 → `staging-2026-09-05`; 09-04 retired so only
one candidate folder exists; walkthrough and rollup paths updated.

## Design discussion: R2.2b, wide-viewport peeks
His proposal from the iPad shots: on viewports that float in horizontal
space, continue the peek out to a full card outline with a gradient
fade "so nothing further is expected past it."

Grounded it in the code before responding: `CanvasPeek` pins a 14px
sliver to the CONTAINER edge (`max-w-md mx-auto`). On a phone that edge
IS the screen edge, so the sliver works by implication — clipped,
therefore continuing. On iPad nothing clips it and the implication
collapses.

Argued his version over the cheaper alternative (pin to the viewport
edge instead), because on a wide canvas the truthful statement is
"there is another deck, over there," which a card shape can say and a
distant sliver cannot. Added two constraints: outline-and-hue only,
never content (content would make it the postponed R2 canvas view
arriving disguised as polish); and the fade must *terminate* rather
than soften, since a hard edge implies another card past it. Raised one
question I can't answer: whether a full outline still reads as *the
afterlife* on the grey variant without a label. Recorded as R2.2b with
all of it.

## Brief processing — both insights were live here

Today's cross-pollination brief carried two findings, and unusually
both landed on code in this repo rather than on theory.

### 1 · The hoisted-lookup trap — already fixed, but *verified* today
Klatch's Daedalus found that hoisting a lookup out of a loop silently
breaks dedup when the loop creates the things being looked up. That is
**exactly** the rc.35 `sourceAdapter` bug: `known` was built from
`deck.cards` before the loop and never grew, so two identical entries in
one batch both imported.

The fix and a comment forbidding re-hoisting were already in. What I had
not done is confirm the test could actually *fail* — and the brief's own
point is that the cleanup-shaped change reverts protection silently, so
a passing test proves nothing on its own. Mutation-tested it: deleted
`known.add(e.externalId)`, both dedupe tests went red naming the exact
assertion; restored, six green. **The guard is real.** Ten minutes to
convert a belief into a fact.

### 2 · "Silent" vs. "silent since when" — a real gap, now closed
PM's cohort found that a monitoring alert reporting silence can't
distinguish *never started* from *practice that died*; the fix is
printing when the thing last ran. Exec's framing: *"the difference
between an instrument and a fire drill."*

The attention rollup had the identical defect and I hadn't seen it. A
🟡 marker says an item is open **now**. It cannot say whether the ask
went up yesterday or five weeks ago — and both deal onto an identical
card. **That is precisely the staleness Xian reported** after living in
the first deck ("some items seemed possibly stale"). He was describing
a missing timestamp; I read it as a missing *audit* and answered with
diligence, which is the weaker fix.

Added `**Since:**` to the four true Open items, dated from git history
rather than invented — `git log -S` on each numbered heading. The
spread is the argument for the change:

| item | since | waiting |
|---|---|---|
| 29 Ted invite | 2026-09-02 | 3 days |
| 26 Ship 1.0 | 2026-08-31 | 5 days |
| 27 Probe feedback | 2026-08-31 | 5 days |
| **10 REQUIREMENTS drift** | **2026-07-28** | **39 days** |

Item 10 has been waiting thirteen times longer than item 29 and looked
the same on the card. Cards now read `waiting N days (since …)`.

Made it a **third structural guard** alongside status and Ask: the probe
exits 1 and names the offender if an open item has no valid `**Since:**`.
Verified by mutation (removed one date → refused, naming item 10;
restored → clean). `ageInDays` exported and unit-tested including month
boundary, leap day, and malformed input — a guard whose null path is
untested is a guard that can't fire.

Pattern worth keeping: **when a user reports a symptom, check whether
they are describing a missing signal rather than a missing effort.**
Xian said "some items seemed stale." I heard "audit more carefully."
The brief made me hear "the card doesn't tell me how long."
