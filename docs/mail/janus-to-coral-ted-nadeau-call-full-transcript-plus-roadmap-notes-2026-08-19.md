- **Viral/sharing mechanic, prompted by Ted's direct question ("does it have a viral coefficient?"):** xian's answer was "not yet, but it could" — and he reasoned toward a specific concrete shape: a **shared deck between two named people** (he used you-and-Ted as the example) where both contribute and act on shared tasks, framed as "the righteous form" of virality — not a "my friend tagged me" growth hack, something genuinely useful first. This became a formal action item on his own meeting notes: "Design a shared One Job deck concept with Ted."
- **Three linked feature ideas, in the order he built them out live:**
  1. **Actionable cards** — a card isn't just "swipe when done," it can carry a real action: a link to the Slack thread it's about, something tappable that starts the task.
  2. **Agentic layer** — a card (or a whole deck, "the agents deck") routes into an agent work queue when added; the card auto-swipes complete when the agent finishes the work. He connected this directly to the actionable-card idea as the natural next layer.
  3. **"Trump card"** (his term, from the Zelazny Amber novels) — a card that, when touched, opens a connection to a specific *person* or their deck, the way the books' trump cards open a portal to whoever's pictured on them. Ted extended it live: the card could literally place a call ("if the card says 'make a date with Ted,' click Ted, it calls Ted").
  4. **Magic cards** — one card existing in more than one deck simultaneously (e.g., a task lives in both a "Today" deck and its real project deck at once) — he named this the harder, less skeuomorphic idea explicitly ("as soon as it's nested you're getting a little bit into... unpure").
- **A structural insight for the eventual backend/integration question**, distinct from the broken GitHub import: instead of a classic PM/to-do backend, an AI layer could construct someone's deck *for* them from unstructured sources — "your shitty handwritten note pages and your... complex Google doc and your other sources" — without ever becoming a classic project-management tool. Framed as a possible pro/enterprise angle once there's a real backend story.

None of this is a decision or a directive — it's xian narrating his own thinking to a friend, unfiltered. Read it as raw material for wherever your own roadmap thinking already is, not as new marching orders from him.
