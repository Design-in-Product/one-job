## What I'm asking for

**Eleven questions about how One Job actually operates.** Below. Answer them in your repo, cc Xian, whenever you have a clear hour — genuinely under an hour; if it's taking longer you're over-investing.

## How to answer, and this part matters more than the questions

- **Answer from how One Job actually works, not how it ought to.** Tidy answers are worse than accurate ones for what this is trying to find out.
- **One sentence per question is a complete answer.** Fragments beat paragraphs.
- **"We don't do this," "we've never needed to," and "I don't know" are real answers.** They're often the most useful ones. Please don't construct something to fill a gap.
- **⭐ If a question doesn't fit One Job — it's confusing, wrongly framed, or assumes something that isn't true — say so and move on.** That flag is worth as much to me as an answer. Some of these questions may simply be bad, and finding that out is part of the point.
- **A short second round of clarifying questions is expected**, so don't try to make the first pass complete.

## One thing I'm deliberately not sending you, and why

**These questions come from a model, and I'm not sending you the model.** Not being cagey, and you're welcome to know it exists — it's the Pimento spine. But if you can see its shape, its categories and what it expects to find, your answers will bend toward it without either of us intending it, and then all we've learned is that One Job can be described in Pimento's vocabulary. Which proves nothing.

**What we actually want is One Job in its own terms.** So: questions now, model later, and I'll show you the whole thing afterward.

## The eleven

1. **What problem is One Job solving, for whom, and how do you know it's real?**
2. **Is there something a piece of work has to line up with before it gets built?** What is it, and who decides?
3. **Where do ideas for One Job live, and how does one get chosen as the next thing to do?**
4. **When you start a chunk of work, what shape does it take?** Does anything run on a regular rhythm?
5. **Once something is underway, how do you know where it stands?**
6. **How does work get from started to in front of an actual user?**
7. **How do you know when a piece of work is done?**
8. **How do you know whether it worked?**
9. **When you need to know something about this project, where do you look?**
10. **Has anything in One Job been removed, killed, or quietly abandoned?** What made that happen — or what would?
11. **What would make you change direction in a significant way?**

## What you get out of it

This isn't extraction. **There are two outputs, and the second one is yours.**

The first is evidence for Pimento — do these questions survive contact with a real, shipped, two-person product.

**The second is guidance for One Job.** Whatever this surfaces that's actually useful to you — and in my experience walking a project through questions like these usually surfaces at least one thing — gets written up and handed to you in this repo. Xian's framing, and I think the right one: the goal is to be useful to One Job, not just to test the edge of a tool. You'll also be in the discussion where we work out what it all means, not just on the receiving end of a summary.

**And to be clear about the direction of scrutiny: the questions are on trial here, not your answers.** If One Job doesn't do something, that's a fact about a working product, and the question that assumed otherwise is the thing with a problem.
