Coral —

**Your precision note is correct and worth keeping raised.** An unqualified "I don't want your data" is falsifiable by anyone who opens the privacy policy, and **a tagline your own policy contradicts is worse than no tagline** — it converts a real strength into a credibility problem, and you only get to discover that in public.

**But I don't think the fix is a qualifier, and I'd rather not water the line down, because it's good.**

## The two readings aren't equally exposed

> *"I don't want your data. Your data is not my business."*

The double meaning Xian confirmed is doing real work, and **the two halves carry different amounts of risk:**

- **The revenue-model reading — "I don't build my business on your data" — is literally, checkably true.** One Job's revenue is pro/free entitlements. Nothing about the business depends on what anyone's cards say. That reading survives any amount of scrutiny because it's a fact about the P&L, not a promise about collection.
- **The colloquial reading — "none of my concern" — is the one that overstates**, because you do want shape-of-use metadata, and you should.

**So the line isn't uniformly risky. It's risky exactly where a reader arrives at the colloquial reading first.** That's a placement problem, and placement is cheaper to fix than wording.

## The placement rule

**Safe unqualified, wherever the business model is the subject:** pricing page, the App Store description near pricing, anything answering "how does this make money," anything about the pro tier. In those spots the reader is already thinking commercially, "business" reads as *business*, and the line is true as stated. **It's a strong line there and I'd use it.**

**Not safe unqualified, wherever data handling is the subject:** privacy policy, onboarding, permission prompts, any settings screen about analytics. There the reader hears "we collect nothing," which isn't true, and the walk-back is public and expensive.

**And for those surfaces you already have better copy than the slogan.** Covenant 7 — *"we measure the shape of use, never the content of it"* — is the sharper line for anyone reading closely. It's specific, it's a real commitment, and it's the kind of sentence that earns trust precisely because it concedes something.

**They're not competing for the same job.** The slogan is a gut-level claim about the business. Covenant 7 is a precise claim about the data. Put each where its reading is the one the reader arrives with, and you don't need a qualifier bolted onto either.

## One thing I'd watch

**The colloquial half is the memorable half**, which means it will travel further than the placement rule can follow it — someone quotes the line in a review or a post, stripped of context, and the collection reading is the one that lands. That isn't a reason not to use it. It's a reason that **covenant 7 should be easy to find from anywhere the slogan appears**, so the precise version is one click behind the punchy one. Cheap insurance, and it only works if it's built in from the start rather than added after someone's already annoyed.
