# The weekly check-in questions

**By:** Coral, 2026-09-15 · **Cadence:** one question a week, per the
deets. Not a survey. No homework. Xian sends; Coral drafts.

**Design rule:** each question must be answerable in one sentence from
a phone, and must ask about something the instrument genuinely cannot
see. Anything R1.5 already counts is not worth a participant's
attention.

---

## Week 1 — the disambiguator

> **When you push a card to later, is it usually "not today" or "this
> one comes back every week"?**

**Why this one first** (Themis's catch, 2026-09-15): the metric cannot
tell those apart. A card deferred eighty times *in avoidance* and one
deferred eighty times *on purpose* produce an identical number, and
Xian's own deck is full of the second kind — he invented recurrence out
of the defer gesture because the app has no recurring cards. Without
this question, deep deferral in the cohort data reads as friction when
it may be the feature working.

It also earns its place as week 1 because it is the only question here
whose answer changes how every later number is read.

## Week 2 — the honest one

> **Did you open it this week? If not, what were you doing instead?**

Non-use is data and the participant must be told so in the asking.
"Instead" is the load-bearing word: it surfaces the competitor, which
is usually a notes app, a text to oneself, or nothing at all.

## Week 3 — the friction one

> **What's the last thing the app made you do that felt like work?**

Deliberately assumes there is one. "Anything annoying?" invites
politeness; this invites a specific answer, and the phrasing gives
permission.

## Week 4 — the one that decides the pilot

> **It's four weeks on. Is One Job still open on your phone, and why
> or why not?**

The retention metric reports whether they used it in days 22–28. It
cannot report *why*, and the why is the whole reason ten people were
asked instead of a thousand.

---

## Held in reserve (use if one above is answered early or falls flat)

- **On the premise:** Has seeing one card at a time changed anything
  about how you work, or is it just a different way to look at a list?
- **On depth:** Have you put cards inside cards? If not, did you know
  you could?
- **On the agentic angle** (pilot ten only, per the positioning
  ruling): Have you dealt yourself a card from one of your agents, and
  did answering from the deck feel better or worse than answering from
  wherever those questions live now?

## What we do NOT ask

- Anything the export already answers (counts, days, depth).
- Anything with a scale ("rate 1–5"). Ten people is a conversation,
  not a survey; a number from ten people is worse than a sentence from
  ten people.
- Anything that implies an obligation to have used it. The deets
  promise no homework; a question that shames non-use breaks it.
